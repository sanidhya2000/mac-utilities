Privacy

Locks your Mac and keeps your work running. Here is exactly what that involves.

What it reads

While a session is armed, AFK reads per-process CPU, disk and network counters — the same figures Activity Monitor shows — so it can tell you what was running and, in "until the work finishes" mode, know when to let go.

What leaves your Mac

Nothing. AFK ships without the network entitlement, so the operating system itself prevents it from opening a connection. There is no analytics, no crash reporting, no update check, and no account system.

What is stored

Your preferences and a summary of the last session (when it started, how long it held, which apps were busy), in the macOS defaults store under com.afkapp.mac.

What it cannot see

AFK has no access to your files, your keystrokes, your screen, or the contents of any application. Process names and resource counters are the limit of its view — the same information any user on the machine can read from Activity Monitor.

Changes made to your system

AFK holds a standard macOS power assertion, which you can inspect at any time with pmset -g assertions. The assertion is owned by the process and disappears the moment the app quits or crashes.

Lid-closed mode is the one setting that outlives the app, because it changes a system-wide flag. That is why it asks for admin approval, and why a watchdog puts it back if the app stops running.
