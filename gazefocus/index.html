The two-second tax on every monitor you own

A second display is supposed to save time. But every time you turn to it, macOS leaves focus behind — so you reach for the mouse, hunt for the window, click, and only then start typing. Dozens of times a day.

⌨️

Typing into the void

You start a sentence on the second screen and discover it went into the chat window you left behind.

🖱️

Mouse round-trips

Every switch is a hand off the keyboard, a drag across two or three displays, and a click.

🪟

Wrong window, right screen

Clicking the display isn't enough — you still have to pick out the window you actually wanted.

How it works

Four steps, about two minutes, and then you can forget it's running.

  1. Allow the camera GazeFocus reads the position and angle of your head from the camera you already have. 720p, 15 frames a second.
  2. Allow Accessibility Moving keyboard focus between other apps' windows is something only macOS Accessibility permission can do.
  3. Calibrate your screens A dot appears at five places on each display. Look at each one, turning your head toward it. Around twenty seconds per screen.
  4. Just work Look at a screen and type. GazeFocus keeps getting more accurate as you go, because every click teaches it where you were looking.

What it does

Focus follows your eyes at three levels — screen, window, and split pane.

🖥️

Switches screens

Detects which display you're facing and moves focus there, restoring the window you were last using on that screen rather than whatever happens to be frontmost.

🪟

Switches windows

Works on a single display too — look at a different window on the same screen and focus follows, no click required.

⌗

Focuses split panes

Inside editors and terminals, GazeFocus focuses the individual pane you're reading, not just the window around it.

🎯

Learns as you work

Every click is treated as a hint about where you were looking. Accuracy improves over days of normal use, with no extra calibration.

⏸️

Knows when to stay out of the way

Never switches mid-keystroke. Waits after you stop typing. Stands down while your hand is on the mouse. Ignores quick glances.

📏

Adapts to how you sit

Compensates automatically when you lean in or sit back, and tells you when it's drifted far enough to be worth recalibrating.

Tuned to not annoy you

An eye-tracking tool that fires too eagerly is worse than no tool at all. Every one of these is adjustable, and the defaults are deliberately cautious.

Settles first

A 300 ms rest filters out quick glances, so looking away briefly doesn't move anything.

Waits for typing to stop

Focus never moves mid-keystroke, nor for ~3 seconds after your last one.

Yields to the mouse

While you're actively pointing or scrolling, GazeFocus stands down entirely.

No edge flicker

A margin at each display boundary stops focus bouncing between adjacent screens.

Ignores looking away

Turn to a colleague or down at paper and nothing happens.

Sleeps when you do

Releases the camera the moment your Mac locks or sleeps — the camera light goes out.

Private by design

The camera is the whole point of the app, so it's worth being precise about what happens to it.

Stays on your Mac

Head position is computed with Apple's own Vision framework, locally. Nothing is uploaded.

Nothing is stored

Frames live in memory for a few milliseconds and are discarded. Nothing is written to disk, ever.

No network at all

The app ships without the network entitlement — it cannot make a connection, accidentally or otherwise.

No account, no analytics

There's nothing to sign up for and no telemetry. It's free, so there's nothing to verify either.

Check it yourself.

The source is public. The entitlements file is four lines long, and network access isn't one of them.

Questions

Is this real eye tracking?

It tracks head pose, not pupils. A webcam can't resolve a pupil accurately enough at desk distance to pick a window — but head angle, combined with where your face sits in the frame, is more than enough to tell which screen you're facing. In practice you turn your head toward another monitor anyway.

Does it work with one monitor?

Yes. On a single display it focuses the window or split pane you're reading. The gain is smaller than with two or three screens, but it's real.

What about glasses, beards, hats, dim rooms?

All fine — it's detecting a face, not identifying you. Very low light is the one thing that degrades it, since the camera needs to see you at all. The menu bar icon tells you when it can't.

Will it fight me when I use the mouse?

No. Any pointer movement stands it down for a moment, and a click is treated as you deciding where focus belongs — it learns from that rather than overriding it.

How much CPU and battery does it use?

The camera runs at 15 fps on a 720p stream, which is a small fraction of one core. You can pause it any time with ⇧⌘G.

Do I need to recalibrate often?

Usually never — it learns continuously from your clicks. It does prompt you if you start sitting at a noticeably different distance, and it resets automatically if you rearrange your displays.

Why does macOS warn me when I open it?

Because it isn't signed with a paid Apple Developer certificate yet. Right-click the app and choose Open the first time. The download page walks through it.
