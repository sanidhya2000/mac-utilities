“Apple could not verify
this app.”

That message is expected, and it does not mean anything is wrong with the app. Here is exactly how to open it.

Why this happens

Apple charges $99 a year for a Developer ID certificate. Without one, macOS refuses to open an app the first time, whoever made it. These apps are free and not monetised, so that certificate has not been bought yet.

You only do this once per app. Everything after that is normal.

macOS 15 Sequoia and later macOS 14 Sonoma

macOS 15 Sequoia, Tahoe and later

Apple removed the old right-click shortcut in macOS 15, so this is now the only route. If someone told you to Control-click and choose Open, that advice is out of date.

  1. Open the app normally Double-click it in your Applications folder. You will get this, which is the expected first step — click Done.
    ⚠️
    “WakeHold” Not Opened
    Apple could not verify “WakeHold” is free of malware that may harm your Mac or compromise your privacy.
    Move to TrashDone
  2. Go straight to Privacy & Security Do this right away — see the note below about the one-hour window.
     Apple menu→System Settings→Privacy & Security
  3. Scroll to the bottom, to “Security” You will see a line naming the app you just tried to open, with an Open Anyway button beside it. Click it.
    Security
    “WakeHold” was blocked to protect your Mac. Open Anyway
  4. Confirm Enter your Mac password or use Touch ID, then click Open Anyway once more in the dialog that follows.
  5. Look in your menu bar That is it. These apps have no Dock icon and no window — they run as a small icon in the menu bar at the top right.
Don’t see the “Open Anyway” button?

It only appears for about an hour after a blocked launch attempt. If the Security section shows nothing, go back and double-click the app again, then return to System Settings straight away.

macOS 14 Sonoma

On Sonoma the older, quicker route still works.

  1. Find the app in ApplicationsDon’t double-click it.
  2. Control-click (or right-click) itChoose Open from the menu.
  3. Click Open in the dialogThis dialog has an Open button; the one you get from double-clicking does not. That is the whole difference.

Still stuck?

“The app is damaged and can’t be opened”

That usually means the download was interrupted, or the file was unzipped by something that stripped it. Download the .dmg again and drag the app straight from the mounted disk image into Applications.

You prefer the terminal

Removing the quarantine flag has the same effect as Open Anyway:

xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/WakeHold.app

Is this actually safe?

Fair question — you should ask it of any unsigned app. Neither app ships with the network entitlement, so macOS itself prevents them from opening a connection. No accounts, no analytics, nothing written anywhere except their own preferences.
