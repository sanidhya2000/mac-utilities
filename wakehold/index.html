How it decides

The same counters Activity Monitor shows, read a few times a minute.

  1. Samples every processCPU, disk and network per app — the real numbers, straight from the system.
  2. Ignores brief spikesOpening a file or loading a page doesn't count. Work has to persist before it registers.
  3. Trusts apps that askBrowsers and editors raise their own "don't sleep" signal during downloads and exports. WakeHold honours it.
  4. Waits, then lets goWhen everything goes quiet it waits out a grace period — so a gap between build steps doesn't drop the hold — then allows sleep.

What it handles

🎬

Knows your tools

Recognises 38 apps across video, downloads, AI agents, development and sync — each with sensible thresholds. Anything unknown still counts if it's genuinely busy.

💻

Works with the lid closed

One-time admin approval, and a watchdog that restores normal sleep within seconds if WakeHold ever stops.

🔋

Battery aware

Stops holding below a threshold you set, and respects Low Power Mode. Never applies to desktops.

🌙

Lets the screen sleep

Only system sleep is held. Your display still goes dark on its own schedule — unless you'd rather it didn't.

🙈

Ignore anything

A VM or sync client that's always busy would hold forever. Click it in the menu to stop watching it.

🪶

Barely there

Well under 1% of one core. It's reading counters, not doing work.
